NJ Security Deposit Rules 2026: What Every Landlord Must Know

By Proactive Property Management · Published July 2026
By Proactive Property Management · July 29, 2026 · Proactive Property Management

What NJ Law Says About Security Deposits

New Jersey's security deposit law — N.J.S.A. 46:8-19 through 46:8-26 — sets strict rules on how much a landlord can collect, where the money must be held, and when it must be returned. Violations can cost a landlord double the deposit in damages plus attorney's fees. Here's what you need to know.

How Much Can You Collect?

For most residential leases, the statutory cap is:

  • Initial deposit: no more than 1.5 times one month's rent.
  • Annual increase: you may collect an additional 10% of the current deposit each year the tenant remains, to keep pace with rent increases — but you must follow the notice rules to do so.

You cannot collect a larger deposit by labeling it a "pet fee," "key deposit," or any other name if the total held exceeds the statutory cap for that tenant.

Where Must the Money Be Held?

New Jersey law requires the deposit to be held in one of two ways:

  • Interest-bearing bank account — the account must be in a New Jersey bank, insured by FDIC or FSLIC, and held separately from the landlord's personal or operating funds; or
  • Money-market fund — the fund must invest exclusively in U.S. government obligations and maintain a $1 net asset value.

Commingling deposit funds with operating money is a statutory violation and can expose you to forfeiture of the right to retain any portion of the deposit.

Annual Notice to Tenants

Within 30 days of the end of each 12-month rental period, you must provide the tenant with written notice disclosing:

  • The name and address of the bank or fund holding the deposit.
  • The account or fund number.
  • The current balance, including accrued interest.

Failure to provide this notice on time forfeits your right to collect the annual 10% increase for that period.

Interest: Who Gets It?

The tenant is entitled to the interest earned on their deposit. You have two options for disbursing it:

  • Pay it to the tenant annually (within 30 days of the anniversary of the tenancy), or
  • Credit it against the next month's rent, with the tenant's written consent.

You may retain 1% per year of the deposit as an administrative fee — this is the only portion of interest you are permitted to keep.

When Must You Return It?

After a tenancy ends, you have 30 days from the tenant's vacate date to return the deposit (plus accrued interest) or provide an itemized written statement of deductions. If the tenant was displaced by fire, flood, or condemnation, the deadline shortens to 5 business days.

The itemized statement must be sent by certified mail or hand delivery to the tenant's last known address or forwarding address. A generic "damages" line does not satisfy the statute — each deduction must name the specific damage, the repair cost, and ideally reference a receipt or estimate.

What Can You Deduct?

Allowable deductions under NJ law include:

  • Unpaid rent or utility charges the tenant owed under the lease.
  • Damage to the unit beyond normal wear and tear.
  • Costs to restore the unit if the tenant left it in a condition that required extraordinary cleaning.

Normal wear and tear is not deductible. Courts consistently define this as deterioration from ordinary use — faded paint, minor scuffs, carpet wear in traffic areas. Attempting to deduct for wear and tear is one of the most common reasons landlords lose deposit disputes.

Penalties for Non-Compliance

If you fail to return the deposit or provide the itemized statement within 30 days, or if you wrongfully withhold any portion, the tenant may sue for:

  • Double the amount wrongfully withheld — this is a statutory penalty, not discretionary.
  • Reasonable attorney's fees and court costs.

New Jersey courts take these deadlines seriously. Even a well-intentioned landlord who misses the 30-day window by a few days can face the double-damages penalty.

Protect Yourself: Document Everything

The single best defense against deposit disputes is a thorough move-in and move-out inspection with dated photographs. Frequently Asked Questions

Can I require first month, last month, and a security deposit?

Yes — "last month's rent" is treated separately from the security deposit under NJ law, so you may collect it in addition to the 1.5× deposit cap. However, the last-month payment must actually be applied to rent, not used as an additional damage fund.

What if the tenant doesn't give a forwarding address?

Send the itemized statement and any refund by certified mail to the tenant's last known address (the unit they vacated). Keep your certified mail receipt. The obligation is to send — not to guarantee receipt — but you must make a good-faith effort and document it.

Does the 30-day deadline apply if the tenant abandons the unit?

Yes. Abandonment does not extend the deadline. The clock starts when the landlord regains possession, which courts typically measure from the date you reasonably knew the tenant had left — not necessarily the lease end date. When in doubt, treat the discovery date as day one.

Can I deduct for professional carpet cleaning?

Only if the carpet condition exceeds normal wear and tear — for example, heavy staining or damage. Routine end-of-tenancy cleaning is generally considered wear and tear and is not deductible. The condition of the carpet at move-in (documented in your inspection report) matters here.

What if the repair costs exceed the deposit?

You may deduct the full deposit and still pursue the tenant in small claims court for the remaining balance. The itemized statement should note that the deposit has been fully applied and state the outstanding amount the tenant owes.
